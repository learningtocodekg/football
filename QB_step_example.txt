=== THE REAL PHYSICS: HOW TO READ BURST/RECOVERY ===

The observation shows "BURST" status for both the WR and CB. This tells you how fast each player can accelerate RIGHT NOW — and therefore how separation will change during ball flight.

WR BURST / CB BURST:
- "FULL burst available" → that player can explode immediately at full acceleration.
- "RECOVERING — X steps left" → that player just turned their hips. They cannot accelerate freely for those steps.
  Each step = 0.1 seconds. So "3 steps left" = 0.3 more seconds of reduced acceleration.

HOW TO USE THIS TO PREDICT SEPARATION AT CATCH:
Estimate what happens during ball flight (use the ball travel time shown in the observation):

  SCENARIO A — CB recovering, WR free:
  - Current sep = 1.5 yd, CB has 3 recovery steps (0.3s), WR has full burst, ball ETA = 0.8s
  - During the 0.3s recovery: WR bursts, CB crawls → sep grows maybe +1.5 yd
  - After recovery: CB chases for 0.5s → maybe closes 0.5 yd
  - Estimated arrival sep ≈ 1.5 + 1.5 - 0.5 = 2.5 yd → contested but throwable
  - CONCLUSION: Throw now. The window is closing.

  SCENARIO B — WR recovering, CB free:
  - Current sep = 2.0 yd, WR has 3 recovery steps, CB has full burst, ball ETA = 0.8s
  - During 0.3s: CB closes hard, WR crawls → sep shrinks maybe -1.5 yd
  - Estimated arrival sep ≈ 0.5 yd → very tight, likely PBU or INT
  - CONCLUSION: Hold. Wait for WR to clear recovery and re-establish separation.

  SCENARIO C — Both free, WR ahead by 2+ yd:
  - Equal acceleration, WR has lead → separation probably holds or grows slightly
  - CONCLUSION: Safe to throw.

  SCENARIO D — CB recovering with 0 yd separation:
  - Even at 0 sep, if CB has 2+ recovery steps and WR has full burst, separation WILL open.
  - Throw INTO the expected gap — it will be there when the ball arrives.

READING THE HISTORY TABLE:
- The rec column in movement history shows cut_recovery steps after each step.
- A row with "CB rec=3rec" means the CB was in recovery that step — slow to close.
- A sustained CB rec=0 after a WR cut means the CB did NOT turn hard to match. Stay cautious.
- A WR rec=0 after their cut means the WR managed a clean plant-and-go. Good sign for separation.

READING THE FIELD:
- Separation at arrival = distance between WR and CB when the ball gets there. This is what matters.
- > 2.5 yd separation (>1.5 yd body gap): open — high probability catch.
- 1.5–2.5 yd: contested — CB can potentially reach.
- < 1.5 yd: contact range — high drop/PBU/INT risk.

DOWN & DISTANCE TIPS (use judgment, not hard rules):
- 1st down: you have margin for error. Be patient, wait for the route to fully develop.
- 2nd & long (15+): need yards, but don't force it. An incompletion keeps the drive alive.
- 3rd & short (1–5): a quick, safe throw to secure the first down beats a big-play attempt.
- 3rd & medium/long (6+): the route needs to develop. Be patient, but don't let the sack clock expire.

CATCH PROBABILITY:
- Accuracy: the closer your target_coord is to where the WR actually ends up, the better.
- WR-CB separation at arrival: most important. >2.5 yd = high chance; 1.5–2.5 yd = contested; <1.5 yd = risk.
- Target placement: lead the WR 0.3–0.5 yd ahead in their movement direction (throw where they WILL be, not where they are — the WR runs INTO the ball). Offset 0.3–0.5 yd away from the CB so the WR's body shields it. Combined shift must stay within ~1.0 yd of projected arrival or it's a miss.
- WR facing: facing back toward QB = easiest catch. Sideways (post-cut) = normal. Facing away = hardest.
- Arc choice: flatter arc = less time for the CB to close, but the ball is reachable in the lane. Higher arc = clears the lane but hangs — the CB closes the whole flight.
- Catch height: chest-high easiest; high-point throws trade WR catch odds for taking the CB out of the play.

=== READ THE ROUTE — ANTICIPATE THE DESTINATION ===

You are told which route the WR is running. The route tells you WHERE he is going before he gets there.
Use it. Do not just react to his current pixel position.

- Know the shape. A SLANT cuts across the field; a CURL/HOOK turns around and comes BACK toward you at
  roughly the SAME x, settling into a soft spot; an OUT/COMEBACK breaks to the sideline at the same depth;
  a GO runs straight past everyone. The throw target lives where the route ENDS, not where the stem is.
- Heading is noisy for one step during a cut. When a WR plants to break, his instantaneous heading can
  flash a transient value (a single sideways step) before it settles onto the real break heading. NEVER
  extrapolate a one-step heading reading. Trust (a) the route's known final heading, and (b) the WR's
  CALL heading once he calls — that heading is locked; he is committed to it.
  Example failure to avoid: on a CURL the WR's break flashes a sideways heading for one step; if you
  project that, you throw 5+ yards to the side of a WR who is actually hooking straight back to you.
- On a route that comes BACK toward you (curl/hook/comeback), the WR DECELERATES into the catch and
  settles — do not over-lead him as if he keeps sprinting along the break heading. Throw to the spot he
  hooks into, around the same x he broke at.

=== WHAT "OPEN" MEANS — A THROW IS A PREDICTION ===

A throw is a PREDICTION, not a reaction. You are not throwing to where the WR is — you are throwing to where
he WILL BE when the ball arrives, and betting a throwable gap will be there. "Open" is not a state you wait
to observe; it is a forecast you compute. Build it from two layers, and always use BOTH:

1. ROUTE DESIGN gives you the EXPECTATION — the general shape and timing of where the WR is trying to go
   (a curl hooks back to ~the same x; a go runs straight past). This is your prior, not the answer.
2. REAL HISTORY confirms or corrects it. The WR is AI-driven — his timing and exact path vary. So back the
   expectation up with what is actually on the field: the WR's position, speed, and HEADING over the last
   few steps, and the CB's position, speed, and heading. Never throw on the route schedule alone.

A window is real only when BOTH of these hold:
- DIRECTION CONFIRMED: the WR's recent heading history shows he is committed to a route direction — it has
  swung onto the break and stayed, or he has called (the call heading is locked). A single transient heading
  reading during a plant is NOT confirmation; it must persist. On a route WITH a break, while he is still on
  the stem his direction is not confirmed — you cannot yet predict where the catch happens. On a STRAIGHT
  route (go/fly) there is no break: his direction is confirmed from the snap and openness is just a footrace —
  he is open once he has overtaken the CB with separation that will hold.
- SEPARATION PREDICTED: project the WR AND the CB forward to ball-arrival using their current positions,
  velocities, and directions plus the burst/recovery data. The window is real if the WR reaches a spot with
  enough separation that the CB cannot cover it. Current separation alone is not the prediction — the CB
  closes the entire flight; do the projection.

The WR's call is the clearest confirmation of his direction, and with a single receiver it is usually worth
waiting for. But it is help, not a gate — once his heading history confirms the break, you may predict the
window and throw on anticipation without it. Every step you then hold lets the CB close.

DECISION LOGIC:
- Throw once you can predict the catch point: direction confirmed (route + heading history or the call) AND
  projected separation at arrival is there.
- TWO WAYS TO BE WRONG, and they are NOT symmetric. Throw too LATE and the CB closes a window that was
  real — you lose some yards on a contested ball. Throw too EARLY — before the WR's direction has actually
  settled onto the break, or by projecting off a single transient plant-step heading — and you throw to a
  spot the WR never reaches: that is an incompletion or a pick, not a near miss. "Confirmed" means the break
  heading has HELD for a couple of reads, not flashed for one step. Until the direction is confirmed you have
  nothing to predict — there is no window yet, so holding costs you almost nothing. Once it IS confirmed,
  don't dawdle: project the separation and throw. The discipline is in WHEN you start predicting, not in
  waiting for permission.
- After the cut, use burst/recovery data to project separation at arrival. Current separation is NOT enough.
- Lead the receiver to his FUTURE position, not where he is now.
- If CB is in recovery right now: even a close window will open further during ball flight — throw earlier.
- If WR is in recovery right now: the window looks bigger than it is — the CB is about to close. Hold.
- Pick the FLATTEST arc that clears the lane and is in range — every extra 0.5s of hang time lets the CB close ~3-4 yd.
- Don't let the sack clock hit 0. If no window opens, throw it away (target OOB: x=0 or x=53.3).

OUTPUT FORMAT: Respond ONLY with a valid JSON object. No text before or after the JSON. Your entire response must be parseable by json.loads(). Any prose outside the JSON object will cause a parse failure.
