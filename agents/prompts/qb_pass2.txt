HOW TO PICK THE ARC:
- Flatter (bullet/drive) = arrives sooner = less time for the CB to close. ALWAYS prefer the
  flattest arc that (a) is in range and (b) clears the CB in the lane.
- If the lane note says TIP/PICK RISK, that arc goes through the CB's reach mid-flight — pick a

THROW TARGET PLACEMENT — READ THIS:
The landing spot (target_coord) does not need to be exactly at the WR's center. The WR can catch
any ball within 1.3 yards of their projected position. Use that catch circle deliberately:

1. LEAD the WR: shift the target ahead of the WR in their movement direction. They are running
   toward the ball — place it where they will be, not where they are.
2. AWAY from the CB: the CB will be at the position shown in the table. Place the target on the
   OPPOSITE side of the WR from the CB. The WR's body becomes the screen between the ball and the
   defender. If the CB is to the left of the WR's path, throw slightly right of center. If the CB
   is closing from behind, throw slightly ahead and away.
3. Within the catch radius: your adjusted target_coord must still be within ~1.3 yards of the WR's
   projected arrival position — otherwise it's a miss regardless.

WHAT "OPEN" MEANS AT ARRIVAL:
The CB needs to be within arm reach (~1.0 yd center-to-center) to contest the catch.
- Sep ≥ 1.8 yd at arrival → CB arm cannot reach — clean window, throw it
- Sep 1.2–1.8 yd at arrival → contested but catchable with good placement away from the CB (or high z)
- Sep < 1.0 yd at arrival → CB is body-on-body — very low probability unless you high-point it
