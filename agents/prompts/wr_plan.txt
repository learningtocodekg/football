PRE-SNAP. This is the ONE moment you get to think for the whole play. You are stateless — once the ball
is snapped you will only be "woken up" at the decision points you write down here, and nothing else you
think now will survive. So externalize your whole plan into a conditional decision tree, in your own words,
grounded in what you'll actually be able to see.

HOW THE PLAN RUNS:
- You author NODES. Each node has an ACTION (a heading + effort you run) and a HOLD (how many seconds to
  run that action before you stop and decide). The engine runs your action for you, with NO further
  thinking, until the hold elapses.
- At the end of a node's hold you are woken up. You re-read the field and pick ONE branch. A branch either
  sends you to another node (to keep developing the route) or CALLS FOR THE BALL (your final break).
- Timing is wall-clock: holds are seconds. This keeps you honest to the route — anchor your first decision
  around the DESIGNED BREAK time you were given. Don't break at t=0.2 on a route that breaks at 1.0s.

CONDITIONS ARE YOURS, BUT GROUND THEM. Each branch's `cond` is your own words for what you'll read off the
CB (e.g. "he's bailing", "he's pressing tight", "he bit my jab"). Future-you must be able to evaluate it
from geometry, so DEFINE it concretely in the cond text — e.g. "cushion = CB 3+yd upfield and backpedaling"
or "tight = CB within 2yd, even with me". Vague labels you can't check are useless to your future self.

KEEP IT SHALLOW. A good plan is 1–3 decision points. The classic shape: stem (hold to near the break) →
read the CB → branch. Branches can differ in WHERE you break, HOW LONG you stem first, or whether you fake.
A fake is ONE node (run a juke heading for a short hold), not a per-tick reflex.

end_route on a `call` branch:
  mode "run"    → keep accelerating that heading (slant/go/out/post/corner/in/drag/double_move/zig/post_corner).
  mode "settle" → run the heading settle_ticks (0.1s) ticks to clear the break, then plant and STOP and wait
                  for the ball (curl, comeback — you come BACK to it).
